# S-0524: Multiplayer trigger completion (remaining stock actions and entry raises)

## Summary

Extends S-0011's non-team action subset for official multiplayer maps, including the loose official maps, and specifies additional object-entry raises. Superweapon grant, set-charge and reset effects belong to S-0512 claims 12 to 15; this spec's action table does not cover those actions. Storm-start and storm-stop actions (44 and 45) belong to S-0513 claim 20, and the waypoint curtain action belongs to S-0513 claim 22: no launching house, ground layer only, without the leaving-stage skip. These rules cover skirmish only; network games, campaigns, the team system and computer-player targeting policy are out of scope.

## Terms

- **The waypoint point.** S-0011 claim 28's cell-centre point at the terrain surface, including its 416-lepton bridge lift. Claims 1, 2 and 4 use a cell, not this point.
- **Demolition warhead.** The rules' demolition-charge warhead, named in Import mapping.
- **Starting-building record.** A snapshot of each house's owned building types and top-left foundation cells, taken before the first tick (claim 9). It records neither health nor tags, production, power-switch state, upgrades or charge.
- **Weather storm.** The weather superweapon's storm, called the *ion storm* in S-0104. S-0513 owns its effects.
- **Entry raise.** An entered-by raise on the entered object's tag, with the entrant as causing object. S-0011 claim 18 supplies event matching and the triggering house; claims 8 to 12 there supply evaluation, repetition and nested-raise suppression.
- **Projectile turn.** One projectile's share of a tick, counted from 1 at its first turn, not from the action's firing tick (S-0306 claim 26).

## Behaviour

1. **Lightning storm at a waypoint (code 102).** An active weather storm blocks this action. Otherwise it requests a storm centred on the waypoint-slot cell, with the imported duration and deferment, owned by the trigger's owner house, possibly no house. A deferred storm does not block another request: the later request replaces the centre, owner and duration. Its remaining deferment is the smaller of the existing positive remaining deferment and the new deferment; if none is pending, it uses the new deferment. A zero deferment starts the storm immediately. The waypoint's bridge lift has no effect on the centre cell. S-0513 claims 14 and 19 own the shared countdown and storm-owner rules; claim 20 owns the other storm controls.
   - Backing: `measurable` (C1). Subsequent storm effects inherit S-0513's backing; they are not newly specified here.
   - Confidence: high

2. **Lightning strike at a waypoint (code 90).** Each action creates a storm cloud at the waypoint-slot cell's cloud point, whether or not a storm is active. Two actions at the same waypoint create two clouds: there is no previous-strike exclusion. Each cloud takes one scenario-stream list pick over the imported cloud-animation list, before its animation-creation draws (S-0202 claims 6 and 10; S-0109 claims 3 and 13).

   Pending clouds produce bolts through the weather effect even with no active storm. Their damage uses the storm owner in force when the bolt occurs, not necessarily the trigger owner or the house in force at cloud creation. With no storm since game setup that owner is absent. Adding a cloud to an active or clearing storm can delay its return to idle until the cloud is no longer present (S-0513 claims 18 and 19). Cloud height follows S-0513 claim 17, not the waypoint-point formula; unsafe animation lists follow S-0513 claim 21.
   - Backing: `measurable` (C2) for repeated clouds, the absent-storm bolt and draw order. Attribution after an intervening storm-owner change, delayed clearing and image-dependent cloud height inherit S-0513 claims 17 and 19's `measurable` backing (scenario SW8).
   - Confidence: high for repeat acceptance and the list pick; medium for bolt processing and attribution

3. **Nuclear strike at a waypoint (code 95).** The action creates a projectile of the fixed-name nuclear payload weapon (Import mapping), with that weapon's damage, projectile type and warhead, no firer and no owning house. The target is the waypoint-slot cell. Its start is 20 000 leptons above the cell centre at the surface height, with a 416-lepton lift on a high-bridge deck cell. Its projectile speed is the engine constant 50 leptons per tick, not the weapon's speed; its initial velocity is the downward vector in Formulas. These are distinct inputs to subsequent flight (S-0306 claims 10 to 15). S-0306 claim 26 gives qualifying vertical-flight predictions: the nuclear wait starts in projectile turn 202 over empty flat ground, turn 199 over a centred stationary ground vehicle, and turn 201 over an empty high-bridge cell at deck height minus 100 leptons. These predictions are conditional on the projectile properties and absence of intervening detonation conditions specified there, not universal timings for every payload configuration. Nuclear-warhead detonation follows S-0306 claim 25. The number slot is unused.

   If no weapon has the fixed name, the original does not safely reject it. Our engine gives the action no effect and the importer warns (departure).
   - Backing: `measurable` (C3; S-0306 claim 26, scenario P40) for the launch inputs, qualifying vertical flight, target, damage, lack of kill credit and unused number slot. These are proposed tests, not completed measurements.
   - Confidence: high for the launch inputs; medium for the subsequent flight hand-over

4. **Create a crate at a waypoint (code 108).** The action uses S-0212 claim 25's single-cell nearby search centred on the waypoint-slot cell: *float* locomotion class if that centre is water, *track* otherwise, *normal* movement zone, no reach region, deck mode off, no height or further test, no no-overlay condition, bridges allowed and no reference cell. The search takes no random draw; its result depends on the action's tick as specified there.

   No crate is placed when all 256 scenario crate places are occupied. Placement also fails if the selected cell is outside the height-aware usable area or holds any overlay; it does not retry another candidate merely because the selected cell has an overlay. A placed crate uses the water-crate overlay on water and the wooden-crate overlay otherwise. Placement redraws an already visible cell but does not explore or reveal it for any house.

   The action works with the crate setting off. A successful placement takes the lifetime draw in Formulas, after any draws caused by overlay placement. With crates off, expiry processing is disabled: an uncollected trigger crate remains even after that lifetime elapses. With crates on, expiry and replacement follow S-0520 claim 4.

   Number-slot value 20 leaves the crate random. Every other value is reduced modulo 256 to a value in 0 … 255. Values 0 … 18 select a fixed crate kind; values 19 … 255 instead use the random pickup selection. S-0520 claim 8 supplies power-up numbering and the random choice; its claims 9 to 25 supply subsequent overrides, conversions and effects. Reduction is exact integer arithmetic, including for negative inputs.
   - Backing: `measurable` (C4) for search, placement, images, settings, lack of reveal, expiry gating, lifetime draw and fixed versus random contents. Power-up numbering and random choice inherit S-0520 claim 8's backing; subsequent effects inherit their respective claims' backing there.
   - Confidence: high

5. **Demolition blast at a waypoint (code 63).** The number slot names a waypoint. At its waypoint point *P* the action deals five successive area damages, each 100 hit points, with the demolition warhead, no attacker and no attacking house (S-0303 claims 1 and 2). The points are *P*, *P* + (85, 85, 0), *P* + (−85, 85, 0), *P* + (85, −85, 0), and *P* + (−85, −85, 0), in integer leptons. Their observable precedence is centre, increasing-x/increasing-y, decreasing-x/increasing-y, increasing-x/decreasing-y, decreasing-x/decreasing-y: a death or trigger action caused by an earlier blast can change what the later blasts hit.

   After those damages, a defined waypoint receives up to four direct bridge-destruction attempts at its cell, stopping at the first success (S-0511 claims 1 and 2). These direct attempts ignore the bridge-destruction setting; bridge effects within each area damage retain S-0303 claim 18's own gate. Thus the bridge can fall even when that setting is off.

   An absent marker still requests all five damages. Its centre is (128, 128, 0) integer leptons when the stand-in cell is blank, not a nearest legal corner cell or a point lifted to that corner's terrain. Cell (0, 0) is never legal on a supported map (S-0009 claims 1 and 2), so the proposed legal-versus-illegal (0, 0) distinction cannot occur. Area damage still follows S-0303 claims 1 and 2: nearby legal targets within the imported spread can be hurt, but damage is not moved onto the map merely because the centre is outside it. There are no direct bridge attempts for the absent marker. S-0009 claim 44 owns the original's shared, mutable stand-in and our blank-cell departure; this claim does not add an absent-marker no-effect departure. Invalid waypoint numbers still follow S-0011 claim 33.
   - Backing: `measurable` (C5 and C11) for damage points, absent-marker requests and direct bridge attempts. Exact precedence among the five damages is `unmeasured`: C5 checks health totals, not a discriminating intervening death/trigger effect. Contaminated stand-in state inherits S-0009 claim 44's `unmeasured` boundary.
   - Confidence: high for amounts, points and direct-attempt count; medium for precedence

6. **Large light flash at a waypoint (code 66).** The number slot names a waypoint. The action requests a drawing-only flash at its waypoint point when the demolition warhead has the bright flag and the presentation gate allows reduced-detail effects: nonzero detail level and the adaptive drawn-frame-rate test. The action itself does not bypass either gate. A positive warhead flash-size fraction supplies the flash-size level as given in Formulas. Otherwise size 300 gives integer level 63 (Formulas). The level is dimensionless, not a radius in cells or pixels; its precise visible footprint is not specified here. Neither the action nor the flash takes a random draw or deals damage. An undefined marker requests the flash at claim 5's absent-marker point under the same gates, not at a substituted legal cell. A request is not a promise of visible pixels: ordinary projection and view clipping still apply. With the blank stand-in its height is 0 leptons.
   - Backing: `measurable` (C6 and C11) for the point, absent-marker request, fallback versus maximum-size image, bright/detail gates and absence of damage or draws. Exact numeric size-level calibration and the visible footprint are `unmeasured`: no independent pixel-to-level calibration is available. The adaptive frame-rate gate is `unmeasured`: C6 deliberately uses a host above its threshold and does not exercise its hysteresis boundaries.
   - Confidence: high for size and gates; medium for the presentation-only classification

7. **Set base ambient light (code 73).** The integer number slot becomes base ambient in hundredths, without division or rounding. It also sets target ambient unless a weather storm is active. The fade and global-effect exceptions are S-0104 claims 4 and 14.
   - Backing: `measurable` (S-0104 scenario L13, using code 73)
   - Confidence: high

8. **Resize local area (code 40).** Action items four to seven become the local area's left, top, width and height, as signed integers in the local area's screen-aligned cell-width/cell-height units, not map-axis cell coordinates (S-0009 claim 4). Subsequent usable-area tests, scrolling and the radar rectangle use the new area (S-0009 claims 4 and 5; S-0101 claim 8). Every legal cell receives terrain recalculation under S-0010 claims 8 and 19; this may create a previously eligible tile animation, not an unconditional restart of existing animations. Pathfinding connectivity is rebuilt for the new area (S-0212 claims 3 and 4). Building radar tracking is refreshed for the new rectangle.

   Shrinking does not remove, relocate, stop or switch off existing buildings or mobile units, and does not clear their orders. Buildings wholly outside and buildings only partly inside have no separate suspension rule: they retain their health, owner and ordinary firing, production, power and defeat-counting rules. In particular an excluded power source still contributes by S-0405 claims 1 to 7, and an excluded building still counts by S-0414 claims 1 to 4. Production progress is not cancelled by the resize, but releasing or placing its product can fail under the new usable-area tests; continued progress does not guarantee a successful exit (S-0404 claims 7 and 13 to 15).

   An object outside is not made untargetable by this action. An otherwise legal attack on it remains an attack; visibility, range and weapon eligibility still apply. A unit outside may receive a move destination inside by S-0214 claim 11. This neither grants passability to excluded cells nor guarantees a route: S-0212 claims 6 and 14 and S-0209 claims 4 and 5 still decide the approach and cell entries. A unit need not be moved inside automatically to remain alive or counted. These rules do not promise that an object clipped out of the current view can be clicked there.
   - Backing: `measurable` (C7 and C12) for rectangle, persistence, firing, production, power, counts and attack/return orders. Animation creation and pathfinding rebuild details are `unmeasured` here: C7 has no animated-tile fixture or connectivity probe that separates a rebuild from updated area tests.
   - Confidence: high for the rectangle assignment and recalculation; medium for subsequent effects

9. **Restore starting buildings (code 130).** Before the first tick, after tag setup and the game-start terrain pass, each house's owned buildings are recorded by type and top-left foundation cell. The per-house restoration order is their surviving object creation order at that snapshot, oldest first, not type order or cell order. For ordinary pre-placed buildings this follows map-entry creation order, including separately created upgrades. An action restores only its resolved house, so records of different houses are not interleaved. Two actions for different houses act in the ordinary action order of S-0011 claim 11. Buildings created during play are not added. The action resolves the number slot as a house value (S-0011 claim 23); an unresolved house gives no effect.

   A recorded cell covered by any ground-layer foundation cell of a building of the recorded type and house receives a healing hit of minus the type's strength, distance 0 leptons, demolition warhead, no attacker or attacking house, neither ignoring defences nor preventing escape. S-0302 claims 5 to 8 and 11 decide its outcome: an ordinary demolishable, unprotected building returns to full health, even with zero versus; immunity can prevent the hit and an undemolishable building can instead lose health. Restoration is not an unconditional health assignment. The covering building need not have its own top-left cell at the recorded cell: a shifted building of the right type and house is healed, and no second building is attempted for that record. A wrong type or owner does not qualify.

   Otherwise a fresh building of that type and house is attempted at the recorded top-left cell, facing 0. Claim 13 gives its state and displacement rules. A blocking building or other placement failure leaves no replacement. Removed foundation overlays are not restored on failure. Restoration supplies no recorded tag to a new building.
   - Backing: `measurable` (C8 and C13) for snapshot, order, covering-cell match, healing and ordinary displacement. Reverse-house snapshot visitation is `unmeasured`: gameplay cannot interleave those snapshots, so C13 checks house separation rather than visitation itself. Failure-side overlay persistence and failed-return removal are `unmeasured`: C14 proposes a blocked fixture, but no guaranteed failed-return fixture has been established. They must not be inferred from ordinary successful placement.
   - Confidence: high for healing metadata, recorded cell and order; medium for forced placement

10. **Engineer capture and tag transfer.** S-0509 claims 2 and 8 give a capture two entry raises: the general building-entry raise and the capture raise. Both precede capture's own ownership and tag changes. Without intervening trigger changes they see the old owner and tag; trigger actions can change that state, and the later raise sees those changes. The general raise can also change which engineer outcome applies (S-0509 claim 3).

    If the engineer still carries a tag whose trigger chain includes the carry-over flag, the captured building receives that tag, replacing its previous tag and dropping one of the old tag's carriers (S-0011 claim 2). Losing its last carrier does not by itself delete the old tag. The carried tag is already attached when capture's ownership change takes effect. Capture gives the building to the engineer's house. The ownership change itself makes no raise on the building's tag: neither the replaced old tag nor the carried new tag receives an ownership-change notification. Without carry-over the old tag remains attached, but receives no such notification either. A house condition or capture-history mark is not an extra building-tag raise (S-0011 claim 8; S-0509 claim 11). Both transfer effects follow the entry raises and precede the engineer's destruction raise (S-0509 claim 8).

    The engineer's own tag gets one destroyed-by-anything raise with the engineer as causing object before the engineer is removed. When the tag carried over, this raise acts on the tag now shared with the captured building; actions selecting that tag's present carriers can affect the captured building. A damage-only attempt has the general entry raise and the engineer's destruction raise, but no second entry raise or carry-over transfer. That outcome cannot occur in skirmish because its enabling rule is always off (S-0509 claims 2 and 6). Capture eligibility belongs to S-0509 claims 3 and 7, not a universal health gate here.
    - Backing: `measurable` (C9; S-0509 claim 8, scenarios E2 and E6) for capture raises, transfer, established ownership precedence and destruction raises. Survival of a replaced tag with no other carrier is `unmeasured`: C9 does not poll that orphan tag independently. Damage-only notifications are `unmeasured` because that outcome is unreachable in skirmish. Carry-over already present at ownership change is `unmeasured`: no building-tag raise occurs in between, and C9 cannot distinguish that internal ordering. Absence of an ownership-change raise is `measurable` (C15).
    - Confidence: high

11. **Vehicle hijacking and tag transfer.** A vehicle thief's takeover raises the vehicle's existing tag with the thief as causing object. That raise sees the vehicle's old owner and tag. The carry-over rule of claim 10 then replaces the vehicle's tag when applicable, and takeover gives it the thief's house and records the thief's type as its hijacker type (S-0312 claim 13). The thief is removed without a destroyed-by-anything raise on its own tag. The crew effects on later vehicle destruction belong to S-0312 claim 13. This claim covers vehicles only. A vehicle-like building instead follows S-0509 claim 17: its entering unit does receive a destroyed-by-anything raise, and any tag carry-over follows the ownership change. Its eligibility, backing and low confidence remain those of that claim.
    - Backing: `measurable` (C9 variant B)
    - Confidence: high

12. **Other object entries.** Garrison entry, infantry entry into its ordered target, sabotage entry into its target building, and vehicle entry into its ordered target building can raise the entered object's tag with the entrant as causing object. The entered-by action effects can therefore occur before the entrant's garrison, sabotage or building-entry effect. This is not a rule that merely receiving an enter order raises the tag, nor that every entry consumes its entrant.

    Spy infiltration is a consuming entry (S-0510 claim 9): the building's entry raise precedes the infiltration effect, and the spy's own tag receives destroyed-by-anything with the spy as causing object after that effect and before removal. This is S-0509 claim 2's same general entry raise, not an extra one. S-0510 claim 1 owns eligibility and its vehicle-like destination exception.

    Recycled infantry and vehicles receive one own-tag destroyed-by-anything raise, with themselves as causing object, after the payments and before removal. Their passengers do not receive that raise merely for being removed with them. This was already specified by S-0603 claims 6 and 10, not a new universal consumption rule. Retained passengers, garrison occupants and absorbed infantry remain objects, not consumed units: entry alone gives no own-tag destruction raise. Claim 14 distinguishes ordinary boarding and service from consuming entries.
    - Backing: `measurable` (C10 and C16); recycling inherits S-0603 claims 6 and 10, scenario G6, and spy notifications inherit S-0510 claim 9, scenario I8. Spy effect-relative state and presence retain that claim's `unmeasured` backing. Sabotage eligibility and uncommon combinations not in claim 14 remain `unmeasured`: no complete data-only fixture list is established.
    - Confidence: high for recycling and retained-object distinction; medium for the classified entry paths

13. **Replacement state and displaced units (code 130).** A missing starting building is a fresh object at full type strength, not the former object's saved state. It uses ordinary during-play placement, not map-loading placement: construction is queued and its build-up follows S-0215 claim 1 and S-0406 claims 14, 15 and 17. A missing build-up does not select a special restoration shortcut; the ordinary one-stage rules apply. The power switch begins on; actual operation, power accounting, factory availability and house superweapon charging follow S-0405 claims 7 and 8, S-0404 claim 8 and S-0512 claims 3 and 4. Restoration starts no saved production job and restores no saved charge. A house's already existing queues and superweapon records are not reset just because this action creates a building. Exact first grant/check ticks remain those of the owning specs, not an immediate-charge guarantee.

    Only ground-layer mobile occupants of the attempted foundation cells are lifted for displacement. This includes a landed aircraft held there on the ground layer; it does not include a bridge-deck occupant, an airborne aircraft or a mobile in an adjacent non-foundation cell. A building or non-mobile blocker aborts the attempt. Previously lifted occupants are offered their original positions on failure, with facing 0; a failed return placement removes that occupant. There is no overlay rollback.

    On successful building placement, displaced occupants are placed one at a time in reverse lifting order. For each, the nearby search is S-0212 claim 25's 1 × 1-cell search centred on its old cell, using its type's locomotion class, movement zone *normal*, no reach restriction, deck mode off, no height, further or no-overlay condition, bridges allowed, and its old cell as reference. Thus the reference-distance rule chooses within the first qualifying ring; this is not a global nearest-cell search. The placement point is the selected cell's terrain-surface centre, without a bridge lift, and facing 0. An infantry placement can choose a standing position there by its ordinary placement rules, but this search refuses a cell already occupied by another displaced infantry unit: they are not deliberately packed into a shared cell.

    Search failure does not leave a unit under the new building or reserve it off the map. The original instead attempts placement using the blank stand-in at cell (0, 0), and removes the unit if that placement fails. Our stand-in follows S-0009 claim 44. A found cell whose placement fails also causes removal. Every re-placed mobile takes the ordinary placement fall-back of S-0215 claims 13 to 15, with its type-specific exceptions; the action does not save and reinstate its old order, nor impose blanket cancellation. A retained destination, queued behaviour or route survives only where those ordinary fall-back rules retain it. Its facing is not preserved. This also applies to a failed building attempt's successful return placements.
    - Backing: `measurable` (C14) for new-object health, construction, switch, ordinary queue/charge treatment, occupant layers, search, placement, facing and fall-back. Exact lifting-order ties between objects in the same cell, contaminated original stand-in behaviour and a guaranteed failed-return-removal fixture are `unmeasured`: C14 does not establish those inputs independently.
    - Confidence: high for constructor state, search arguments and facing; medium for resulting construction/operation and mobile placement effects

A positive entry raise uses the entrant as causing object, on the destination's tag as it exists at that raise. It is an arrival notification, not an order-acceptance notification. None of these paths adds an entered-by raise merely on leaving. A later fresh entry can notify again. The counts below are raises, not guaranteed trigger firings: S-0011 claims 8 to 12 still govern matching and latches.

Engineer/spy consumption and vehicle-thief exceptions remain claims 10 to 12; none can be generalized to every object leaving the map. Eligibility, approach, acceptance and retained passenger state stay with S-0507 claim 6, S-0314 claims 16 to 18, S-0501 claims 21 to 25, S-0402 claims 9 to 13, S-0311 claim 12 and S-0605 claims 6 to 8.
    - Backing: `measurable` (C16). Recycling additionally inherits the already-approved S-0603 claims 6 and 10, scenario G6; absorption already has S-0605 claim 6, scenario A3. These requests do not reopen those established consuming-versus-retaining classifications.

## Formulas

**Trigger crate lifetime (claim 4).** Use S-0202 claim 6's unit-interval draw *u*, not a raw value times 2⁻³¹. It takes R(0, 2147483646), including any rejected raw values. With imported double-precision regeneration field *C* in game minutes:

> *L* = RZ₅₃(450 × *C*), *H* = RZ₅₃(1800 × *C*)
>
> *T* = trunc(RZ₅₃(*L* + RZ₅₃(RZ₅₃(*H* − *L*) × *u*))) ticks

RZ₅₃ denotes double-precision rounding under S-0205 claim 5 (`quirks.chop_rounding`); trunc converts to a signed integer toward zero. The original uses floating point at each multiplication, subtraction and addition. For positive finite *C* within the integer conversion domain, the bounds before rounding are half and twice *C* game minutes, with 900 ticks per game minute. Lifetime assignment does not imply expiry while crates are off. Out-of-domain conversions follow S-0205 claim 7's common-form wrap and huge/non-finite-result rules; there is no added clamp.

**Flash-size level (claim 6).** Without a positive imported size, exact integer arithmetic gives

> *r* = min(63, max(21, ⌊300 × 64 / 256⌋)) = 63 flash-size levels.

For positive imported single-precision fraction *f*, level = trunc(RZ₅₃(63 × min(*f*, 1))) (dimensionless). The original multiplies in floating point under S-0205 claim 5, then uses claim 7's common integer conversion. This path has an upper bound of 63 but no lower bound of 21.

**Nuclear start and velocity (claim 3).** Start = target surface-centre point + bridge lift + (0, 0, 20 000), in signed integer leptons, with no rounding. Let *p* be the stored double constant 1.5707483884323126 radians (slightly below π/2). Let *S*(*p*) and *C*(*p*) denote, respectively, the sine and cosine values supplied by S-0105 F1's table trigonometry. Initial velocity components, in double-precision leptons per tick, are

> *V*ₓ = RZ₅₃(RZ₅₃(*C*(*p*) × *C*(*p*)) × (−100))
>
> *V*ᵧ = RZ₅₃(RZ₅₃(*S*(*p*) × *C*(*p*)) × (−100))
>
> *V*_z = RZ₅₃(*S*(*p*) × (−100)).

The table's cosine at this angle is not exactly zero, so the horizontal components are tiny but not exactly zero. Subsequent movement and integer position conversion are S-0306 claims 10 to 15's, not a constant-speed 400-tick fall inferred from the stored speed of 50.

**Blast displacements (claim 5).** Adding (±85, ±85, 0) uses signed integer leptons, no rounding. Ambient assignment (claim 7), action rectangle items (claim 8) and crate-kind reduction (claim 4) also use integer arithmetic only.

## State and ordering

| State | Event | Result |
|---|---|---|
| no active or deferred storm | code 102 | request storm at waypoint cell |
| storm deferred, not active | code 102 | later centre/owner/duration, no extension of the remaining positive deferment |
| storm active | code 102 | no effect |
| any storm state | code 90 twice at one waypoint | two clouds; their strikes follow S-0513 claim 18, subject to claim 21's unsafe-list departures |
| crates off | code 108 succeeds | crate appears and is not removed by lifetime expiry |
| recorded building still present, same type/house | code 130 | healing hit, subject to ordinary damage rules |
| recorded building absent or different | code 130 | attempted replacement |

Observable precedence:
- Code 63's five damage effects precede its direct bridge attempts. With the game's bridge-destruction setting off, a deck occupant can be damaged before the direct attempt drops the deck.
- A code-90 cloud's list pick precedes its creation draws, so the recorder identifies those draws in that order.
- Capture's two entry raises precede its own ownership/tag changes; trigger actions can change the state seen by the later raise (S-0509 claims 2 and 8). Vehicle hijack entry precedes takeover. The consumed engineer's destruction actions can already act on the newly tagged captured building.
- Spy entry actions precede the infiltration effect; the spy's destruction raise follows the effect and precedes removal (S-0510 claim 9).
- A building created after the first tick begins is absent from the restoration record.

## Edge cases and quirks

- **Failed restoration return** (`quirks.restoration_failed_return_removes_unit`, default on, original behaviour). If forced building placement fails and a displaced unit cannot be put back, it is removed. With the switch off, failed restoration preserves displaced units by retaining them for deterministic nearby placement rather than deleting them. The exact original failed-return fixture remains unmeasured (claims 9 and 13); the successful-path search is now specified in claim 13.
- **Restoration and healing protections.** Claim 9 delivers an ordinary healing hit, not unconditional restoration. S-0302 claim 7 owns the undemolishable-healing quirk and its alternative.
- **Repeated code-90 actions.** Repeated actions at the same waypoint are accepted by default. `quirks.repeated_lightning_strike_suppressed` (default: off) controls this compatibility behaviour. When on, after one cloud has been created by code 90 at a waypoint, further code-90 actions at that waypoint create no cloud.
- **Crate settings.** By default, trigger-crate placement ignores the crate setting, while expiry processing is disabled when crates are off. `quirks.trigger_crate_respects_setting` (default: off) makes code 108 have no effect when crates are off. `quirks.trigger_crate_expiry_while_disabled` (default: off) allows an uncollected trigger crate to expire while crates are off, after its assigned lifetime. These switches affect only the stated behaviours.
- **Empty storm animation lists.** S-0513 claim 21 owns these invalid-data departures. An empty cloud list divides by zero in the original; our engine makes no cloud and warns. An empty bolt list is also unsafe in the original: our importer warns, the cloud-height term is 0, and strikes make no bolt animation but otherwise happen.
- **No nuclear weapon.** Claim 3's safe no-effect departure requires an importer warning, not a quirk switch. An absent blast/flash marker is not suppressed: claims 5 and 6 use the stand-in point, subject to S-0009 claim 44's existing departure.
- **Old tag without carriers.** Tag transfer changes carrier counts but does not itself delete the replaced tag. Ordinary firing and expiry remain S-0011's.

Unsafe missing-weapon, empty-animation-list and missing-overlay outcomes have `unmeasured` backing and medium confidence: no proposed scenario deliberately runs the original into unsafe data. Their warning/no-effect alternatives are design, not measured original behaviour.
