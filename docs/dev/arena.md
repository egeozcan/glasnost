### The oracle arena (step 4c, second part)

**The records.** An oracle arena record is a measurement record `measurements/<topic>/M-xxxx-*.md` with a CSV of the same stem beside it whose header is exactly `row,col,row_side,seed,winner,end_frame` (one line per game: row and column type, the row's side `A` or `B`, seed 0 to 5, winner `row`/`col`/`none`, the sample frame at which the game was over; the records give no army shares since their revision of 2026-10-29). The Setup's `- Map:` line names the map in back quotes and gives its `sha256`. `oracle::load_records` reads every such pair; `arena_records_parse` (CI) checks that each parses and has 6 seeds per side per pairing, and fails on a record CSV with any other header. Today: M-0001 (Yuri vehicles, 96 pairings), M-0002 (Yuri infantry, 24), M-0003 (Allied and Soviet vehicles, 35); 1860 games in all.

**M-0017 (M8 step A3).** The comparison leaves out the 7 MIND against E2 games of M-0001 that crashed in the stock game (`oracle::crashed`: winner `none` with an end frame short of the limit sample; our games of the same side and seed are left out too), so that pairing is judged on its 5 side-B games, n = 5, as the record's Tolerance says. The report also splits its totals into pairings whose stock rows M-0017 confirms and the SREF and V3 pairings it does not cover (`oracle::UNCONFIRMED_TYPES`); since intake 47 those are shown, not scored. Ledger: [m8-claims/intake-M-0017.md](../m8-claims/intake-M-0017.md).

**The setup we play (measure: M-0001 to M-0003, Setup and Procedure).** The record's map, read from `GLASNOST_GAME_DIR` and checked against its sha256 (never committed), with its rules layer; W = start waypoint 2. Side A plays the first country at start 0, side B the second at start 1, superweapons and crates off, game speed fastest (index 0). Since intake 87 the game start is S-0216 r67 claim 8's *Game start* and *Removal before the groups* (Q-2336): the houses are a human player house at start 3 (`oracle::HUMAN`, M-0001's idle house; side A's country, `oracle::HUMAN_COUNTRY_SIDE`, a design rule of S-0216 r69 claim 8, Q-2446 answered), side A, side B (`oracle::SIDE_HOUSES`), then the neutral and special houses, all created in two rounds of S-0202 claim 14's house-creation draws; the bases setting is on and the unit-count setting 0, so each of the three player houses gets its base unit and nothing else. After `new_game` (after resource-glint setup) and before the first group, `oracle::take_off_before_the_groups` takes every vehicle the human house does not own out of the game (the computer houses' base units and the neutral house's vehicles) with `objects::take_out_quietly`: no draw, no animation, the cells freed, the creation numbers kept. The human house's base unit stays on its base cell all game; its house has `House::idle_test_house` set, so no target search takes its objects (`acquire::candidate`), no hit damages them (`damage::apply`), no unit retaliates against them (`acquire::retaliate`) and no house's anger grows towards it (`acquire::anger`, so it never becomes an enemy house). Because the human house is a contender that no other house is allied with, the game no longer ends (`econ.end.finished`) when one side is gone; the arena's sample loop still ends it at the next sample. Since intake 90 (S-0216 r69 claim 8, *Seeds and the human player house's country*, Q-2447 answered) the game with the records' seed number *s* seeds the scenario stream with 1000 + *s* (`oracle::game_seed`); the CSVs, the reports and every command-line seed argument keep the seed number. So seeded, our first creation draw takes the raw value at M-0019's *N*₀ for each of the six seeds (706, 709, 711, 718, 706 and 701 raw values; `arena::m0019_the_first_creation_draw_takes_the_raw_value_at_n0` passes). With the stream seeded with *s* itself it missed by +5, −11, −9, −9, +5 and +7. Per side round(9000 / cost) units (an exact half to even, as the CSV denominators showed before the records' revision; kept while Q-2099 is open), at least 1, infantry at most 60, unit *i* on (W.x ∓ 6 ∓ ⌊i/8⌋, W.y − 4 + (i mod 8)), A facing east, B west; every unit has the hunt behaviour queued and no order is given (S-0216 r37 claim 8). Samples every 304 frames; the game is over at the first sample where at most one side owns anything, or at the first sample at or after frame 6000. A side's share (the old CSVs' last two columns, gone since the revision) was the summed cost of what its house owns at the end over its group's cost at the start. The houses are test-only computer-controlled player houses (`human = false`; glossary revision 58, S-0215 revision 69 Terms, S-0309 revision 23 *Test-only exception*): the records' houses are hard computer players, so ours take the original's computer-owner rules everywhere the neutral and special houses do, with the oracle house's skill level (the rules' maximum, `[IQ] MaxIQLevels`, S-0412 claim 3) and difficulty index 0, and no teams; hard also gives the easy row through the handicap (S-0412 claims 4 and 5), and as computer players they stop the unused-house cleanup (S-0009 claim 28). No brain of ours is attached.

**Setup differences** (what we cannot or do not reproduce; each can move a result):

- The original's skirmish computer players were in the stock games; in ours no AI runs (our own AI takes no part, D14), and any effect the stock computer players had on the hunting units is absent.
- No observer slot and no tech-level setting: our skirmish setup has neither, and nothing in the arena reads the tech level.
- `--ai-houses` plays the houses as human-controlled AI houses of our engine instead (the earlier default); it changed which pairings pass (below) and is kept for comparison.
- The row type's group is created first, on whichever side it plays, so it is first in the update order (S-0203 claim 3). This is S-0216 r59 claim 8's *Order* (intake 77), which M-0019 measures as the records' creation order; until intake 77 side A's group came first.
- The placement is S-0216 r59 claim 8, a rule of our own (intakes 71 and 77): each unit is created, tested and placed before the next. Each cell is tried with a new try of the unit's type, created with its whole creation set (its slot objects and slaves). A cell the take test refuses (outside the usable area by the height-aware test, an entry outcome other than *clear*, a unit there for a vehicle, the first-row refusals or a full cell for infantry) keeps the try's creation draws and, for infantry, an unused R(0, 3); the try's objects are discarded with their creation numbers reserved (`objects::discard_unplaced`, the documented departure: the original leaves an off-map object), and the next of the eight neighbours in claim 8's order is tried (`oracle::place_unit`). The set-up stops with an error if none takes the unit. Infantry take their standing-position draw; *hunt* is queued. `Arena::first_raw` is the place of the first creation draw, which claim 8's acceptance item compares with M-0019's *N*₀ (`m0019_the_first_creation_draw_takes_the_raw_value_at_n0`, local lane). M-0006's placed cells and M-0019's tick-0 findings are acceptance checks, not overrides. The facings agree with S-0216 claim 1.
- Our end read: when the game finishes, the state is read at the next multiple of 304 frames, as the procedure samples.
- Rocket missiles in flight (a launcher's spawned missiles) are not counted as owned objects (`oracle::owned`); counting them gave launcher shares above 1 that the stock CSV does not show.

### The fragment record M-0021 (M8 step A3, intake 93)

M-0021 is not a pairing table. It plays one test pairing (F: a tank whose invisible shot bursts into 3 fragments; G: a two-stage gattling tank) 16 times on M-0001's ground, and states per-shot, per-stage and per-run rows. `glasnost-headless arena --record M-0021 [--threads N] [--out DIR]` (`oracle::run_m0021`, `glasnost_tools::oracle_m0021`) builds the test types on a tank type of our choice (`GLASNOST_M0021_BASE`, default `MTNK`; the projectile is the first stock invisible projectile that can hit ground and has no fragment, airburst or cluster weapon, or `GLASNOST_M0021_PROJECTILE`). It plays the record's parts A, A0, B and the control, and judges each row of the Result by the record's Procedure. `--record all` runs it after the other records. The verdicts go to stdout. The report with the record's local values (shot counts, *h*, health per hit, the expected gaps) goes to the git-ignored `DIR/oracle-M-0021.txt`, which is never committed. The local-lane test is `arena::m0021_rows_hold_in_our_engine`. The recorder reads `Effect::Shot`'s target and `Effect::Hit`'s warhead and health, which are presentation fields and not hashed.

At intake 93, 22 of 24 rows pass, including every per-shot, gap and stage row. Two rows fail. F's centre distance at its first shot is 1504 leptons in our runs against the record's 1527. Part B's G-dead count differs in 2 of 6 runs. The record's Notes say both rows depend on how the vehicles move, and Q-2516 asks about the parts of the setup the record leaves out. [m8-claims/intake93.md](../m8-claims/intake93.md) has the ledger and the interim rules.

At intake 95, the record's answers to Q-2516 replaced the interim rules (parts A, A0 and the control play on to tick 608, the observer owns one building and no vehicle, F's group is created first, G's numbered weapons as built). The same 22 rows pass and the same two fail with the same numbers; Q-2536 asks what remains. `GLASNOST_M0021_OBSERVER` names the observer's building type (default: the first unarmed 2 by 2 building that is no factory or gate). [m8-claims/intake95.md](../m8-claims/intake95.md) has the ledger.

At intake 97, the record's answers to Q-2536 and Q-2546 added its reading points and seed rows to the harness. The per-tick rows are stamped *t* = read after tick *t* has run, as before, and now also hold every vehicle's health (`Recording::rows`, every vehicle that is still an object of the world, dead or alive). Our shot event carries no positions and the tool cannot stop inside a tick, so the recorder derives the positions of a shot's own row (`oracle_m0021::shot_positions`): the firer's position after the tick (a vehicle's firing step follows its movement in its own turn, S-0310 claim 9), and the target's position after the tick when its turn comes before the firer's in the update order read at the start of the tick (S-0203 claim 3), else its position before the tick. ### The synthetic arena

**The scenario.** Two groups, A and B, each a list of type IDs and counts (infantry or vehicles), meet on our own flat map (`testkit::empty_map`: clear, flat cells, no objects, no EA bytes). The houses are hostile player houses in a sandbox setup (no start positions, starting units or bases; not AI houses), of the scenario's countries, else both of the rules' first country that is not passive. Each group stands in rows of 5 cells across the map's x axis, one unit per cell; the middle cells of the two front rows are `start-distance` cells apart, centred on the map's middle cell; further rows stand one cell further back; every unit is placed by S-0216's play-time placement (`testkit::spawn_in_play`, since M8 step A3 intake 21): it faces the nearest facing value of the direction to the enemy front cell, an infantry unit stands at the position its draw gives, and a unit rests in its resting behaviour until the order tick; a refused placement is an error of the scenario. **Alternation:** in a trial with an odd seed group A is house 0, stands on the lower-x side and is created and ordered first; a trial with an even seed is swapped, group B taking all four (`arena::swapped`). Being created first puts a group first in the update order (S-0203 claim 3), so it takes its turns first; the two sides differ in their geometry (with an odd start distance the fronts are not equally far from the middle, with an even one the middle cell is crossed differently from the two sides). Unalternated, these favoured one group of a mirror matchup by up to 100 % (see the findings below); alternating balances both over an even number of trials, starting unswapped with the default first seed 1. In the first tick in which orders are taken, every unit is ordered to attack-move to the enemy front cell through `orders::issue` (an order a person could give); every 60 ticks after that, each idle unit (guard or area guard with no target and no destination) is ordered to attack-move to the cell of the nearest enemy unit. A unit *of a group* is one of the objects created for it that still exists with health above 0 and is still owned by its house (a unit under mind control counts for no group; a lifted or carried one still counts). A trial ends after the first tick at whose end a group has no units (the other wins; neither: a draw), or as a timeout `tick-cap` ticks after the order tick. **Time to kill** = ticks from the order tick (the first) to the end tick, both included. **Survivors** = units of each group at the end, and their summed health in per-mille of the group's summed health at creation. Seeds are the simulation seed: trial *i* uses seed `first-seed + i`. Row width, the re-order period and the defaults (map 32, distance 10, cap 6000 ticks, 32 seeds, the rules' lobby game speed) are our tool's choices.

**Running it.**

```sh
# Synthetic ruleset (the default; the synthetic combat types CART, CRUSHER, BUGGY, WALKER):
cargo run --release -p glasnost-tools --bin glasnost-headless -- arena --seeds 32 --ticks 6000 "A:CART*2" "B:WALKER*4"
# Options: --first-seed K, --size S, --distance D, --speed G, --countries X,Y, --synthetic (ignore GLASNOST_GAME_DIR),
#          --watch (shots per group, the firing check's answers per group and slot, and every unit's final state in the first trial), --types (list the type IDs a matchup can name).
# Reference ruleset of a real install (local only; also writes the git-ignored local-lane/arena.txt):
GLASNOST_GAME_DIR=<install> cargo run --release -p glasnost-tools --bin glasnost-headless -- arena --types
GLASNOST_GAME_DIR=<install> cargo run --release -p glasnost-tools --bin glasnost-headless -- arena --seeds 32 "A:<type>*5" "B:<type>*5"
# Every fenced-block arena record's matchup (the alternative format below; none exists today):
GLASNOST_GAME_DIR=<install> cargo run --release -p glasnost-tools --bin glasnost-headless -- arena --records [--seeds N]
```

The reference ruleset is the local lane's import (the game's search order, rules and art, temperate theater, no map layer), resolved; a stock map is never loaded, so no map's rules layer changes the matchup. The import's default game mode is skirmish, so the skirmish game-mode layer is applied: the arena's rules are a skirmish's without the map layer. Each matchup prints N, the wins of A and B in per cent, draws and timeouts, the mean, standard deviation, minimum and maximum of the time to kill over the decided trials, the survivors of each group (mean units and mean health in per-mille, with their standard deviations), and every trial as a record trial line with its seed. 32 trials of a 5-against-5 matchup take about 3 s (release). Which matchups are available: any pair of infantry or vehicle type IDs of the loaded rules (`arena --types` lists them with kind, tech level and owning sides; on the reference ruleset that list is local output only). Matchups run in M7b on the reference ruleset (Yuri infantry and vehicles against Allied and Soviet ones, N = 32) are kept in `local-lane/m7b/arena/`, git-ignored; type IDs of the stock rules are not written in this file.

**Arena measurement records (alternative format).** No record uses this form today (the lead's records use the CSV form above); it is kept, documented, for small matchups on the synthetic arena's flat map. `arena --records` reads every record `measurements/<topic>/M-xxxx-*.md` (see `measurements/TEMPLATE.md`) that holds a fenced block with the info string `arena`. Each block is one matchup, one `key: value` per line (`#` starts a comment):

| Key | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `group-a`, `group-b` | yes | The groups: `TYPE*n, TYPE*n` (type ID and count; count 1 when `*n` is left out) |
| `country-a`, `country-b` | no | The houses' countries (default: the rules' first country that is not passive, for both) |
| `map-size` | no | The map is `map-size` × `map-size` cells (default 32) |
| `start-distance` | no | Cells between the two front cells (default 10) |
| `tick-cap` | no | Ticks from the order tick before a trial is a timeout (default 6000) |
| `game-speed` | no | The lobby game speed, 0 to 6 (default: the rules' lobby speed) |
| `n` | yes | Number of trials; must equal the number of `trial` lines |
| `wins-a`, `wins-b`, `draws`, `timeouts` | no | Cross-checks: must match the trial lines when given |
| `trial` | n lines | `<winner> <ticks> <survivors-a> <survivors-b> [<health-a> <health-b>]`: winner `A`, `B`, `draw` or `timeout`; time to kill in ticks (for a timeout, the cap); units of each group left; optionally their health in per-mille of the group's full health. Integers only. |

The record's procedure must say how the stock game was set up to match this scenario (the same groups, placement, map and orders), including the game mode (the arena applies the skirmish game-mode layer) and the alternation: the trial lines in order, the 1st, 3rd, … with group A as the first house, on the lower-x side and created first, the 2nd, 4th, … with group B so; an even N. Per-trial lines are needed: the KS test compares the distributions of the times to kill, which a mean and a spread cannot give. When the front matter says `value-visibility: local`, the outcome keys (`n`, the win counts, `trial`) may come instead from the git-ignored `measurements/local/<id>.json`: `{"arena": [ {"n": 32, "wins-a": 20, "trial": ["A 812 3 0", …]}, … ]}`, one object per `arena` block in order, with the same keys (integers or strings; `trial` an array of trial-line strings); a key may be in the record or in the local file, not in both. Win rates under stated settings are not rules data, so arena records will usually be `committed`.

**The comparison of the alternative format.** `arena --records` runs each fenced-block record's matchup on the reference ruleset over the record's N seeds (`--seeds N` overrides), and tests, at α = 0.01 per test (`arena::ALPHA`, our test design choice): each group's win rate with a two-proportion z test (pooled variance; equal rates with a pooled variance of 0 give p = 1), and the times to kill of the decided trials with a two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test (ties handled; asymptotic p-value with Stephens' correction, λ = (√nₑ + 0.12 + 0.11/√nₑ)·D; no comparison when neither side has a decided trial, a failure when only one has). It prints, per matchup, both rates, the p-values and pass or fail. `arena_records_parse` (in CI) also checks that any record in this form parses. The statistics are unit-tested against hand computations in `stats.rs`; `records_are_run_and_compared` runs the whole record path on the synthetic ruleset over invented records in a temporary folder.

**Findings of the first reference runs (M7b step 4c, N = 32).** Outcomes vary with the seed (standard deviations of the time to kill of 9 to 69 ticks), so the KS test has material. The first runs found two engine defects and one arena defect, all fixed in step 4c. (1) **Infantry whose fire frame equals its firing sequence's frame count never fired** (two stock infantry types, even one against one: every trial a timeout). The tick ran the completion follow-up of S-0107 claim 8 before the firing rules of claim 11, so the stage never equalled the fire frame; S-0107 *State and ordering* puts the firing rules before the completion follow-up ("judged against the sequence that is current before that tick's completion follow-up"; "When the fire frame equals *C*, the shot is still released, on the tick the sequence completes"), and the follow-up before the movement rule. The infantry turn is now fear rules (`infantry::fear_turn`), firing (`behave::infantry_step`), then completion and movement (`infantry::sequence_turn`); tests `s0107_fire_frame_equal_to_the_count_still_fires` and `s0107_attack_state_ends_the_tick_after_completion` (`claims_s0301.rs`; both fail with the old order). The determinism goldens did not change. (2) **A weapon whose projectile is named in another letter case had no projectile**, so its shots did no damage (one stock vehicle type: 38 shots, the target group at 1000 ‰), and against a heavy tank it took no target at all (0 shots; with the fix it fires). The importer copied a weapon's `Projectile` and `Warhead` as written; S-0004 claims 2 and 13 make them type references that find the existing type without regard to letter case. They are now references, written as the named type's key (test `weapon_references_ignore_letter_case`, `crates/convert`). A local check of the reference import found no case mismatch in the other name-only fields that the simulation looks up by exact name (`DeploysInto`, `UndeploysInto`). (3) **Mirror matchups were not even** before the alternation (above): synthetic, N = 512, three carts a side, B won 74 %; reference, N = 256, five of one infantry type a side B 55.9 % and three of one tank type a side B 82.4 %. Swapping only the sides and swapping only the creation order gave exactly mirrored counts (226 to 30 out of 256 for the carts), so the house order plays no part; the place and the creation order do, in a direction that depends on the type (one walker a side favoured the lower-x side). With an odd start distance the fronts are not equally far from the middle and the farther side won every trial (carts, distance 9). Being created first means taking turns first (S-0203 claim 3), which is game behaviour; the geometry is the arena's. With the alternation, B's rate at N = 256: carts 50.8 %, five walkers 52.3 %, three crushers 46.1 %, five crushers 51.6 %; reference, five infantry 49.2 %, three tanks 54.3 %, five of a second infantry type 45.7 % (all within 1.4 standard errors of 50 %). The results with the fixes, and the outputs from before them, are in the git-ignored `local-lane/m7b/arena/` (`summary.txt`, `before-fixes/`).
